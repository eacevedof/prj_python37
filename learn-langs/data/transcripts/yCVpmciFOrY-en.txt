Well, the first three we're going to discuss are created based on the adverb HEEN.
So the second part is HEEN.
And the word HEEN in itself means different things.
For example, that you are "away".
"Hij is ver HEEN" = "Hij is ver weg."  (He is far gone.)
For example, if someone is drunk, he is far gone.  ("Hij is ver HEEN").
He is no longer completely there, mentally...
It can also mean that you are "going somewhere" and that it is "on the way" to something.
For example: "We rijden HEEN en terug."  (We drive there and back.)
Yes, HEEN = "the way to somewhere".
Or it could mean "going in a certain direction."
"Waar wil je HEEN?"  (Where do you want to go (to)?)
You can also say: "Waar wil je NAARTOE?"
Well, the three adverbs based on HEEN are: DOORHEEN, OMHEEN and OVERHEEN.
DOORHEEN you can translate to "through".
For example:
"Ik loop dwars DOOR alle mensen HEEN."  (I walk through all the people.)
So, there are a lot of people and I walk THROUGH those people.
Or for example:
"Ik kijk DOOR jou HEEN."  (I see through you.)
Yes, that's actually not possible...
But with some devices you can see THROUGH something.
You can see what's inside or behind it because you're looking THROUGH it.
"En jaarlijks varen hier duizenden boten DOORHEEN."  (And thousands of boats pass through here every year.)
So, for example, through this lock or through this river:
"Jaarlijks varen daar duizenden boten DOORHEEN."
You can also use this word figuratively, for example:
"Ik begrijp waar je DOORHEEN gaat."  (I understand what you are going through.)
This is no longer literally "going through something" but more in life, through situations, so it is already a bit more figurative.
But even more figuratively (so not at all literally) is, for example:
"Hij zit er helemaal DOORHEEN."  (He's had it.)
So he's been through so much, he can't take anymore!
He's had it... It's done!
"Hij zit er DOORHEEN."
Then OMHEEN.
Well, OM usually means "around"
so OMHEEN is the direction (and motion) "around".
For example:
"Ik loop OM de lamp HEEN."  (I walk around the lamp.)
Or: "De tafel waar wij allemaal OMHEEN zitten..."  (The table we are all sitting around...)
So that is literal.
And you can also use it figuratively, for example:
"Ergens OMHEEN draaien."  (To beat around the bush, not being direct.)
So don't say directly what you mean,
but "je draait OM het onderwerp HEEN".
(To beat around the bush).
Or: "Nee, we kunnen niet OM jou HEEN."  (No, we can't ignore you.)
So we can't ignore you.
So literally: "I can't go around you" = that won't work, I'll bump into you.
We can't ignore you!
And then OVERHEEN.
Well OVER, that also means "over" in English.
So for example:
"Ik heb een bank met een kleed EROVERHEEN."  (I have a sofa with a rug over it.)
So the rug is OVER the couch.
"Het ligt EROVERHEEN."  (It's over it.)
ER here refers to "de bank" (the couch).
Or: “De bal ging OVER het net (HEEN)."  (The ball went over the net.)
It didn't go into the net.
"Hij ging OVER het net HEEN."  (It went OVER the net.)
"Hij ging EROVERHEEN."  (It went over it.)
ER again refers to "het net".
You can also say: "De bal ging OVER het  net."
So with HEEN...
We will see that later (this is not the case with the other adverbs),
but often adverbs with HEEN (such as OVERHEEN)
then you do not always have to use HEEN, which can sometimes be optional.
But as soon as you got ER in the sentence, you have to add HEEN!
Then it is really necessary!
Or: "Ik spring OVER het boek"  (I jump OVER the book)
or "OVER het boek HEEN".
Yes, again optional.
"Ik spring OVER het boek (HEEN)."
"Ik spring EROVERHEEN."  (I jump over it.)
Then you must add HEEN (if ER refers to "het boek").
"Ik spring EROVERHEEN."
And for a moment...
If you want to know more about ER, how ER can sometimes replace a word,
watch this video because I explain it there.
I'm not going to explain that now, that's a very extensive topic.
But you sometimes see that we can replace words with ER.
You can also wipe something along the surface, for example:
"Ik haal een doekje OVER de tafel HEEN."  (I'll wipe a cloth over the table.)
Yes, I clean the table with a cloth:
"Ik haal ER een doekje OVERHEEN."
You can also use this figuratively by saying, for example:
"Ik ben nog niet OVER mijn eerste liefde HEEN."  (I'm not over my first love yet.)
No, I'm still heartbroken...
"Ik ben ER nog niet OVERHEEN."  (I'm not over it yet.)
"Ik ben nog niet OVER mijn eerste liefde HEEN."
You see that these words sometimes separate
and that is if you also use the word to which this adverb refers in the sentence.
"Ik loop DOOR alle mensen HEEN."
"Ik loop ERDOORHEEN."
Not with ER.
But: "Ik loop DOOR de mensen HEEN."
"Ik loop OM de lamp HEEN."
"Ik loop EROMHEEN."
Then it's together again!
"Het kleed ligt OVER de bank HEEN."
"Dit is een bank met een kleed EROVERHEEN."
"Ik haal een doekje OVER de tafel HEEN."
"Ik haal ER een doekje OVERHEEN."
Or you can say:  "Ik haal een doekje EROVERHEEN."
So you see: with nouns (if you have those in the sentence)
then one part comes before and another part after.
And if you have ER in the sentence, then the adverbs stay together.
And we will see that later in all other examples too
This doesn't only apply to ER, but also to  HIER, DAAR, WAAR & ERGENS (and similar words).
Well, before we go to the next adverbs based on other words,
there are also a few adverbs on HEEN that don't necessarily fit into this list for a specific movement,
but that you can make based on HEEN, such as:
WAARHEEN or ERHEEN or DAARHEEN or HIERHEEN,
which gives you some kind of direction.
So not necessarily a movement such as DOORHEEN and OMHEEN, but more of a direction.
For example, with WAARHEEN you ask for a direction:
"WAAR ga je HEEN?"  ("Where are you going to?)
"DAAR ga ik HEEN!"  (I'm going there!)
"HIER ga ik HEEN..."  (I'm going here...)
Yeah, so DAARHEEN is for far away and HIERHEEN is for near.
And in cases like these, you can also replace HEEN with NAARTOE.
That's not possible with the other three.
You can't say OMNAARTOE and DOORNAARTOE and OVERNAARTOE...
No! So those are really different types of adverbs with HEEN.
But with this one you can also say NAARTOE.

Well, the next four were made with DOOR.
And DOOR usually also indicates a movement: "through something".
You can use DOOR on its own, for example:
"Ik rijd DOOR een tunnel."  ("I'm driving through a tunnel.)
We just saw that you can also combine DOOR with HEEN.
That was the very first one we saw: DOORHEEN.
Yes, but now we are looking at four adverbs that end in DOOR.
For example BINNENDOOR.
BINNENDOOR means that you don't go around anything (outside).
No, you go through the inside! So, for example:
"Ik ga niet om het huis lopen, ik loop BINNENDOOR."  (I'm not walking around the house, I'm going "through inside.)
Yes, inside through the house to the other side.
Or: There are two ways we can get to the beach by car...
"We kunnen via deze weg OM de stad HEEN...)  (We can go around the city using this road...)
...of we kunnen BINNENDOOR rijden."  (...or we can take a shortcut.)
And then you often have to drive THROUGH something, THROUGH the busy city for example.
Then we have ONDERDOOR. Well, that literally means that you go UNDER something.
Yes, for example:
"Kun jij daar ONDERDOOR doorkruipen?"  (Can you crawl under that?)
"Kun je ONDER de tafel DOOR kruipen?"  (Can you crawl under the table?)
Or figuratively you can also "go under something".
When it destroys you.
For example:
"Maria en Frans zijn vorig jaar gescheiden..."  (Maria and Frans got divorced last year...
"...en Maria ging daar bijna aan ONDERDOOR!" (and it almost destroyed Maria!)
Yes, she found it so difficult... It hurted so much!
"Ze ging ER bijna aan ONDERDOOR! (It almost destroyed/killed her!)
Then TUSSENDOOR.
Well, TUSSEN is usually literally that there is something here and here, and I am BETWEEN two things.
(Yes, now BETWEEN two hands.)
And TUSSENDOOR is a movement you make:
moving BETWEEN two or more things, literally.
For example:
There are all these people...
"...en ik loop TUSSEN alle mensen DOOR."  (...and I walk through all the people.)
"Ik loop ERTUSSENDOOR."  (I'm walking between them.)
But you can also use TUSSENDOOR for "in the meantime".
So between appointments or things in time, on a day, that you have planned, for example.
"Het is vandaag een lange dag en ik moet heel veel doen...  (Today is a long day and I have a lot to do)
"...maar ik probeer TUSSENDOOR snel wat te eten."  (...but I'm trying to eat something quickly in between.)
Yes, "TUSSEN alle afspraken DOOR" (between all the appointments)
I try to eat something quickly.
"Ik probeer TUSSENDOOR wat te eten."
And the funny thing is that something you eat in between
(so not really a meal, not lunch, not dinner, not breakfast, but more of a snack)
is what we also call "een tussendoortje" in Dutch.
A snack is "een tussendoortje" that you quickly eat BETWEEN all the things you are doing.
And the last one with DOOR is RECHTDOOR.
Yes, that is when you are going in a certain direction and you are not going to the right or to the left
(the other way around for you: right & left)
but RECHTDOOR (straight).
"Je loopt RECHTDOOR"  (You walk straight on.)
In a straight direction forward.
So for example:
"Je moet hier RECHTDOOR lopen." (You have to walk straight on here.)
Yes, walk STRAIGHT...
"Gewoon RECHTDOOR lopen!"
And then there is one more on DOOR that is not really indicates a direction,
but you use this when you leave somewhere,
and that is ERVANDOOR.
So: "Dag allemaal, ik moet ER weer VANDOOR..." (Hello everyone, I have to leave...)
Or: "Ik ga ER weer VANDOOR!"
So: "Bye, I'm leaving!"
Or a little more figuratively:
"Ze is ER met het geld VANDOOR." (She ran away with the money.)
Sh*t! She stole the money and she left.
"Ze is ER met het geld VANDOOR."
Now two that start with OM, namely: OMHOOG and OMLAAG.
Well, OM itself has a lot of meanings (too many to list right now).
I also couldn't really see how it related to OMHOOG and OMLAAG, so we're not necessarily going to look at that.
But HOOG is "high" and LAAG is "low".
Yes... HOOG, LAAG...
And OMHOOG is towards "high" (so "upwards")
and OMLAAG is towards "low", "down".
So OMHOOG is the direction "up" & OMLAAG is "the direction down".
"Ik kijk OMHOOG."  (I'm looking up.)
"Ik wijs OMHOOG."  (I'm pointing up.)
Or: "Ik kijk OMLAAG."  (I'm looking down.)
Or: "Ik wijs OMLAAG"  (I'm pointing down").
Or for example:
"De lift gaat OMHOOG en de lift gaat OMLAAG"  (The elevator goes UP and the elevator goes down.)
Two others for a motion and direction are: OP and VANAF.
Yeah, so that's similar to OMHOOG and OMLAAG, except now you're actually going ON an object,
or you're actually going going OFF an object.
For example jumping or something.
For example:
"Ik stap OP het boek." (I step on the book.)
“Ik stap EROP"  (I'm stepping on it.)
(Yes, so OP is actually the only one which is just one word now.)
Or, “Ik stap VAN het boek AF.  (I'm getting off the book.)
“Ik stap ERVANAF."  (I'm getting off.)
“Het meisje loopt de trap OP." (The girl is walking up the stairs.)
She goes upstairs: "Ze loopt de trap OP."
Or: "Het meisje loopt de trap AF naar beneden."  (The girl walks down the stairs.)
"Ze loopt VAN de trap AF."
"Ze loopt ERVANAF.”

Okay, that's RECHTSAF & LINKSAF."
And then we have VOORUIT and ACHTERUIT.
And UIT can be used in many ways on its own
but you can also use it on its own in relation to a destination and a movement.
For example: "Welke kant ga jij UIT?"  (Which way are you going?)
"Moet jij ook die kant UIT?"  (Do you have to go that way too?)
Or: "De vrouw zit voor zich UIT te staren."  (The woman is staring out in front of her.)
She was staring in a certain direction.
So you can use UIT in that way.
But also in combination with with VOORUIT (in a forward direction)
and ACHTERUIT (in a backward direction).
So for example:
"Ik loop & kijk VOORUIT."  (I walk & look forward)
And then ACHTERUIT. Well, for example:
"Ik loop ACHTERUIT."  (I walk backward.)
Or: "Ik kijk ACHTERUIT."  (I look backward.)
But you can also use it figuratively, namely:
"We gaan VOORUIT!"  (We are moving forward!)
Yes, our business is going well, it is getting better!
"We gaan VOORUIT!"
"De gezondheid van die man wordt steeds slechter..." (That man's health is getting worse and worse...)
"...dus zijn gezondheid gaat ACHTERUIT." (...so his health is going backward.)
So “VOORUIT gaan” figuratively is “getting better”
and “ACHTERUIT gaan” figuratively is “getting worse.”
And finally just four with LANGS.
Well, you can use LANGS on its own and it means that you "go past" ("along" or "past").
“Ik loop LANGS de stoel." (I walk PAST the chair.)
Well, and then we have four adverbs: VOORLANGS, ACHTERLANGS, BOVENLANGS and ONDERLANGS.
(Something like that...)

Yes, I'll raise them along the front.

"Ik breng mijn armen ACHTERLANGS omhoog."  (I raise my arms from the back.)
VOORLANGS & ACHTERLANGS.
